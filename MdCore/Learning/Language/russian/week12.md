# Day 3 — What Is Verb Aspect?

Russian verbs have a grammatical distinction called:

> **вид глагола**

verb aspect

There are two major aspects:

> **несовершенный вид**

imperfective

and:

> **совершенный вид**

perfective

At A1, do not try to learn every aspect rule.

Instead, understand the central idea.

---

# 1. Imperfective

Imperfective commonly focuses on:

* an action in progress
* repeated activity
* habitual activity
* an activity without emphasizing completion

Examples:

> Я читал книгу.

I was reading a book.

> Я каждый день читал книгу.

I read a book every day.

> Я буду читать книгу.

I will be reading/read a book.

---

# 2. Perfective

Perfective commonly presents an action as:

* completed
* achieved
* occurring as a whole
* reaching a result

Example:

> Я прочитал книгу.

I read the book / I finished reading the book.

The focus is the completed result.

---

# 3. Compare

> Я читал книгу.

I was reading/read the book.

Focus: activity.

> Я прочитал книгу.

I read/finished the book.

Focus: completion.

This distinction is extremely important in Russian.

---

# 4. Common Aspect Pairs

| Imperfective | Perfective | Meaning    |
| ------------ | ---------- | ---------- |
| читать       | прочитать  | read       |
| писать       | написать   | write      |
| делать       | сделать    | do/make    |
| смотреть     | посмотреть | watch/look |
| слушать      | послушать  | listen     |
| покупать     | купить     | buy        |
| говорить     | сказать    | speak/say  |
| повторять    | повторить  | repeat     |
| изучать      | изучить    | study      |
| начинать     | начать     | begin      |
| заканчивать  | закончить  | finish     |

Do not assume every Russian verb forms its pair in an obvious way.

Learn aspect pairs together as vocabulary.

---

# 5. Repeated vs Completed

Compare:

> Я каждый день читаю книгу.

I read every day.

Repeated activity.

> Я прочитал книгу.

I finished the book.

---

# 6. Present Tense

One important rule:

**Perfective verbs generally do not describe a current ongoing action in the same way imperfective verbs do.**

For example:

> Я читаю книгу.

I am reading a book.

But:

> Я прочитаю книгу.

I will read/finish the book.

The perfective form has future meaning in this context.

This is why aspect is so important.

---

# 7. Future

Imperfective:

> Я буду читать книгу.

I will be reading/read the book.

Perfective:

> Я прочитаю книгу.

I will read/finish the book.

For this course, focus on recognizing this difference rather than mastering every nuance.

---

# 1. читать / прочитать

> Я читаю книгу.

I am reading a book.

> Я прочитал книгу.

I finished the book.

> Я буду читать книгу вечером.

I will read the book this evening.

> Я прочитаю книгу вечером.

I will finish/read the book this evening.

---

# 2. писать / написать

> Я пишу письмо.

I am writing a letter.

> Я написал письмо.

I wrote/finished the letter.

> Я буду писать письмо вечером.

I will write/be writing the letter this evening.

> Я напишу письмо вечером.

I will write the letter.

---

# 3. делать / сделать

> Что ты делаешь?

What are you doing?

> Я делаю домашнее задание.

I am doing homework.

Completed:

> Я сделал домашнее задание.

I did/finished the homework.

Future:

> Я буду делать домашнее задание вечером.

I will do/be doing homework this evening.

> Я сделаю домашнее задание вечером.

I will finish/do the homework this evening.

---

# 4. смотреть / посмотреть

> Я смотрю фильм.

I am watching a film.

> Я посмотрел фильм.

I watched the film.

> Я буду смотреть фильм.

I will watch a film.

> Я посмотрю фильм вечером.

I will watch the film this evening.

---

# 5. покупать / купить

> Я покупаю хлеб.

I am buying bread.

> Я купил хлеб.

I bought the bread.

> Я буду покупать продукты.

I will buy/be buying groceries.

> Я куплю хлеб.

I will buy the bread.

---

# 6. Useful Conversation

**— Ты читаешь эту книгу?**

Are you reading this book?

**— Да, я читаю её сейчас.**

Yes, I'm reading it now.

**— Ты уже прочитал её?**

Have you already finished it?

**— Нет, ещё не прочитал.**

No, I haven't finished it yet.

This introduces:

> уже = already

> ещё = still/yet

These are extremely useful A1 words.

---

# 7. уже / ещё

> Я уже прочитал книгу.

I have already read the book.

> Я ещё читаю книгу.

I am still reading the book.

> Я ещё не прочитал книгу.

I haven't read the book yet.

The last pattern is especially useful:

> **ещё не + perfective**

# 4. Cases

At A1 you have encountered the six major Russian cases:

| Case          | Core question | Main A1 function            |
| ------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- |
| Nominative    | кто? что?     | subject                     |
| Accusative    | кого? что?    | direct object               |

| Dative        | кому? чему?   | recipient/experiencer/need  |

You do not need every ending and exception to be automatic yet.

# 6. Aspect

At A1, learn common pairs as vocabulary rather than attempting to derive every pair systematically.

# Essential A1 Connectors

Your next step toward natural Russian is connecting sentences.

Learn:

| Russian    | Meaning      |
| ---------- | ------------ |
| и          | and          |
| но         | but          |
| или        | or           |
| потому что | because      |
| поэтому    | therefore/so |
| тоже       | also         |
| ещё        | still/yet    |
| уже        | already      |
| сначала    | first        |
| потом      | then         |
| после      | after        |
| до         | before/until |
| иногда     | sometimes    |
| обычно     | usually      |
| часто      | often        |
| всегда     | always       |
| никогда    | never        |

Example:

> Сначала я работаю, потом я учусь.

First I work, then I study.

> Я изучаю русский, потому что мне нравится язык.

I study Russian because I like the language.

> Я работаю дома, но иногда я работаю в офисе.

I work from home, but sometimes I work in the office.

# What Comes Next

The 3-month course gives you an **A1 foundation**. The natural next stage is not simply learning more isolated vocabulary. It is expanding the same system toward **A2** through:

1. deeper case usage
2. complete motion-verb system
3. broader verb aspect
4. adjective and pronoun declension
5. past/future aspect combinations
6. commands and requests
7. comparative and superlative forms
8. conjunctions and subordinate clauses
9. more natural conversational patterns
10. listening comprehension
11. longer reading
12. spontaneous speaking
13. Russian word formation
14. increased vocabulary
15. authentic Russian media

The central transition is:

> **A1: Я могу сказать простую мысль.**

I can express a simple thought.

> **A2: Я могу объяснить, связать и уточнить мысль.**

I can explain, connect, and clarify an idea.

Your next phase should therefore focus increasingly on **fluency, accuracy, listening, aspect, and sentence expansion**, rather than introducing grammar in isolation.
