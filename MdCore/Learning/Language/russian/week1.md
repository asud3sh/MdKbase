# DAY 1 — Cyrillic Foundations: First 10 Letters

2. Learn your first 10 Russian letters.
3. Recognize familiar-looking letters that represent different sounds.
4. Read a few short Russian words.
5. Begin developing a direct association between Cyrillic and sound.

---

## Lesson 1.1 — Understanding the Russian Writing System

Russian uses the Cyrillic alphabet.

Modern Russian has **33 letters**:

* 10 vowels.
* 21 consonants.
* 2 signs: the hard sign and the soft sign.

Russian is written from left to right.

Unlike English, Russian spelling provides relatively consistent information about pronunciation. However, pronunciation is influenced by stress, consonant softness, and the sounds surrounding a letter.

For example:

| Russian | Meaning |
| ------- | ------- |
| мама    | mother  |
| папа    | father  |
| дом     | house   |
| кот     | cat     |

You will notice that some letters look familiar.

But be careful.

The Russian letter **Р** represents an R sound, not the English P sound.

The Russian letter **Н** represents an N sound, not the English H sound.

This is one of the most important beginner challenges.

### Key Pronunciation Notes

**Б б** — Similar to English B.

**Г г** — Usually a hard G, as in *go*.

**Д д** — Similar to English D, though Russian consonant articulation differs in detail.

**З з** — Similar to English Z.

**И и** — Similar to the vowel in *see*. It often occurs after soft consonants.

**Л л** — Russian L may be hard or soft. You will study this distinction further.

**П п** — Similar to English P, but generally unaspirated compared with English P.

**У у** — Similar to the vowel in *boot*.

**Ф ф** — Similar to English F.

**Я я** — At the beginning of a word, it is approximately "ya." After a consonant, it commonly indicates that the consonant is soft and represents an /a/-type vowel.

## Lesson 2.3 — The First Personal Pronoun

### Я — I

The word я means "I."

Examples:

**Я.**
I.

**Я — Анна.**
I am Anna.

**Я — Мария.**
I am Maria.

The dash in these introductory sentences is a conventional way to represent the omitted present-tense form of "to be."

You do not need to memorize a separate Russian word for "am" in these sentences.

This will become an important grammatical principle in Week 2.

For now, learn the expression as a complete pattern:

**Я — [name].**

## Lesson 3.2 — The Most Important New Sounds

### Ж ж

This is a voiced fricative, similar to the sound in the middle of the English word *measure*.

Example:

**журнал** — magazine

### Ц ц

This represents a /ts/ sound.

Example:

**цирк** — circus

### Ч ч

This is similar to English "ch."

Example:

**чай** — tea

### Ш ш

This represents a relatively hard "sh" sound.

Example:

**школа** — school

### Щ щ

This is generally pronounced as a soft, long sh-like sound in standard modern Russian.

Example:

**ещё** — still / more

Do not worry about perfecting the distinction between Ш and Щ on Day 3. Listening practice will help develop it.

### Х х

This is a throaty fricative, similar to the sound in Scottish *loch*.

Example:

**хорошо** — good / well

### Ы ы

This is one of the most challenging Russian vowels for English-speaking beginners.

It has no exact English equivalent.

It is generally produced with the tongue positioned farther back than for И.

Example:

**мы** — we

Do not force an English vowel into this position. Listen and imitate native-speaker recordings.

### Э э

This is an /e/-type vowel without the initial y-like quality associated with Е at the beginning of a word.

Example:

**это** — this / it is

### Ю ю

At the beginning of a word, Ю is approximately "yu."

Example:

**Юра** — a male given name

After a consonant, Ю commonly signals softness and an /u/-type vowel.

### Й й

This is a consonant, not a full vowel.

It represents a short y-like sound.

Example:

**йогурт** — yogurt

### Ь and Ъ

These are signs, not independent vowel or consonant sounds.

The soft sign commonly indicates that the preceding consonant is soft.

The hard sign separates a consonant from a following iotated vowel in particular contexts.

Their pronunciation and spelling rules will be developed gradually throughout the course.

## Lesson 4.1 — Hard and Soft Consonants

One of the most important features of Russian pronunciation is the distinction between hard and soft consonants.

A soft consonant is generally produced with the tongue raised toward the hard palate. This is called **palatalization**.

A hard consonant lacks this secondary palatalized articulation.

Compare:

**мат** — mat
**мать** — mother

The final consonant in мать is soft, indicated by the soft sign.

The distinction between hard and soft consonants can change the meaning of a word.

### How Softness Is Commonly Indicated

A consonant is often soft before:

* Е е
* Ё ё
* И и
* Ю ю
* Я я

A soft sign (ь) can also indicate softness.

However, these are introductory patterns. There are spelling rules and exceptions that you will learn as the course progresses.

### Practice Pairs

| Hard | Soft  |
| ---- | ----- |
| мат  | мать  |
| угол | уголь |

Do not expect to master every hard/soft distinction today.

The goal is to become aware that consonant softness is meaningful in Russian.

---

## Lesson 4.2 — Word Stress

Russian stress is mobile and must often be learned with the word.

Compare:

**ма́ма** — mother
**окно́** — window
**молоко́** — milk

Stress is important because it affects vowel pronunciation and may distinguish otherwise similar forms.

For this course, always learn a new word together with its stress.

When stress is not marked in ordinary writing, consult a dictionary or instructional recording when uncertain.

### Stress Practice

Read these words slowly:

* ма́ма
* па́па
* окно́
* молоко́
* хорошо́

Say each word three times.

Then listen to a native-speaker recording and compare your pronunciation.

---

## Lesson 4.3 — Vowel Reduction

In Russian, unstressed vowels are often pronounced differently from their stressed counterparts.

For example:

**молоко́** — milk

The written letter О is pronounced most clearly in the stressed syllable. Unstressed О vowels are reduced, often toward an /a/-like sound in standard pronunciation.

This means that Russian pronunciation cannot be learned by giving every written vowel its full, stressed sound.

### Beginner Rule

For now:

1. Identify the written vowels.
2. Identify the stressed syllable.
3. Listen to the word.
4. Imitate the recorded pronunciation.

Do not attempt to calculate every reduced vowel independently yet.

---

## Lesson 4.4 — The Shadowing Method

Shadowing is a listening-and-speaking technique.

### Procedure

1. Listen to a short recording.
2. Listen again while reading the Russian text.
3. Repeat the phrase immediately after the speaker.
4. Repeat without looking at the text.
5. Record your own voice.
6. Compare your recording with the original.

Use short phrases rather than long recordings.

For Week 1, practice:

* Привет!
* Как дела?
* Спасибо.
* Меня зовут Анна.
* До свидания!

Replace Анна with your own name when practicing.

### Day 4 Completion Criteria

You should understand what hard and soft consonants mean, recognize stress in familiar words, and be able to imitate several short Russian phrases.

## Lesson 5.4 — Formality in Russian

Russian distinguishes between informal ты and formal or plural вы.

You do not need to master the grammar of these pronouns today.

However, understand the basic social distinction:

* Ты — informal singular "you."
* Вы — plural "you" or formal singular "you."

When unsure, Вы is generally the safer choice with unfamiliar adults in formal situations.

This distinction will be developed further in Week 2.

## Lesson 6.3 — Basic Conversation Structure

A simple Russian introductory conversation can follow this sequence:

1. Greeting.
