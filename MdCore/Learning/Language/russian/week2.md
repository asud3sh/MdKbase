# 2. DAY 1 — Personal Pronouns & The Missing "To Be"

## Today's objective

Understand how Russian expresses identity and basic facts without using a present-tense equivalent of "am," "is," or "are."

By the end of today, you should be able to say:

* I am a student.
* You are a teacher.
* He is a doctor.
* We are friends.

You will also learn the personal pronouns needed to construct these sentences.

## Lesson 2.1 — Russian Personal Pronouns

Russian has six basic personal pronouns, plus a distinction between informal and formal/plural "you."

| Russian | Pronunciation aid | English                         |
| ------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------- |
| я       | ya                | I                               |
| ты      | ty                | You (informal singular)         |
| он      | on                | He                              |
| она     | a-NA              | She                             |
| оно     | a-NO              | It                              |
| мы      | my                | We                              |
| вы      | vy                | You (formal singular or plural) |
| они     | a-NEE             | They                            |

### Understanding ты and вы

Russian distinguishes between informal and formal forms of "you."

**Ты** is generally used with:

* Friends.
* Family members.
* Children.
* People with whom you have an established informal relationship.

**Вы** is used:

* When addressing more than one person.
* In many formal situations.
* When addressing an unfamiliar adult politely.

Examples:

Ты — Анна?
Are you Anna? (informal)

Вы — господин Иванов?
Are you Mr. Ivanov? (formal)

For now, learn the distinction as a social and grammatical pattern. You will study the full grammar of pronouns later.

---

## Lesson 2.2 — Russian Has No Present-Tense "To Be" in Ordinary Identity Sentences

In English, we say:

* I am a student.
* She is a doctor.
* They are friends.

In Russian, the present-tense equivalent of "to be" is normally omitted in these sentences.

| English            | Russian       |
| ------------------ | ------------- |
| I am a student.    | Я студент.    |
| She is a doctor.   | Она врач.     |
| He is a teacher.   | Он учитель.   |
| We are friends.    | Мы друзья.    |
| They are students. | Они студенты. |

Notice that Russian does not require a separate word for "am," "is," or "are."

### The Basic Pattern

**Subject + Noun**

Я + студент
I + student

Я студент.
I am a student.

Она + врач
She + doctor

Она врач.
She is a doctor.

This is called a zero copula: the present-tense linking verb is not expressed in ordinary sentences of this type.

### Important Distinction

Russian does have a verb быть (to be).

However, in ordinary present-tense identity sentences, it is generally omitted.

You will encounter быть in the past and future tenses later in the course.

---

## Lesson 2.3 — Identity Sentences

Learn the following expressions.

| Russian          | English                  |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ |
| Я студент.       | I am a student.          |
| Я студентка.     | I am a female student.   |
| Он врач.         | He is a doctor.          |
| Она врач.        | She is a doctor.         |
| Он учитель.      | He is a teacher.         |
| Она учительница. | She is a female teacher. |
| Мы друзья.       | We are friends.          |
| Они студенты.    | They are students.       |

Some profession nouns have different masculine and feminine forms. Others, such as врач, can be used for people of different genders.

For now, learn the forms as vocabulary. You will revisit noun gender and agreement throughout the course.

---

## Lesson 2.4 — Forming Negative Sentences

Russian uses **не** (not) before the word or phrase being negated.

Examples:

Я не студент.
I am not a student.

Она не врач.
She is not a doctor.

Он не учитель.
He is not a teacher.

Мы не друзья.
We are not friends.

### Pattern

**Subject + не + complement**

Я не студент.

Do not add a present-tense form of быть.

Incorrect for an ordinary present-tense identity sentence:

Я не есть студент.

Correct:

Я не студент.

# 3. DAY 2 — Noun Gender: Masculine, Feminine & Neuter

## Today's objective

Understand one of the most important organizing principles of Russian grammar: grammatical gender.

By the end of today, you should be able to identify the likely gender of many Russian nouns and recognize why gender matters for adjectives, pronouns, and past-tense verbs.

---

## Lesson 3.1 — What Is Grammatical Gender?

In Russian, every noun belongs to a grammatical gender category.

There are three:

1. Masculine
2. Feminine
3. Neuter

Gender is a grammatical property of the noun. It does not always correspond to the biological sex of a person or animal.

For example:

| Russian   | Meaning        | Gender    |
| --------- | -------------- | --------- |
| стол      | table          | Masculine |
| книга     | book           | Feminine  |
| окно      | window         | Neuter    |
| студент   | male student   | Masculine |
| студентка | female student | Feminine  |

The grammatical gender of a noun affects other words associated with it.

You will gradually see this in adjectives, pronouns, and past-tense verbs.

---

## Lesson 3.2 — The Three Main Gender Patterns

### Masculine Nouns

Many masculine nouns end in a consonant.

Examples:

| Russian     | Meaning    |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| дом         | house      |
| стол        | table      |
| студент     | student    |
| врач        | doctor     |
| город       | city       |
| университет | university |

A noun ending in a consonant is often masculine, but this is a general pattern, not an absolute rule.

### Feminine Nouns

Many feminine nouns end in -а or -я.

Examples:

| Russian   | Meaning        |
| --------- | -------------- |
| книга     | book           |
| мама      | mother         |
| сестра    | sister         |
| студентка | female student |
| неделя    | week           |

### Neuter Nouns

Many neuter nouns end in -о or -е.

Examples:

| Russian | Meaning  |
| ------- | -------- |
| окно    | window   |
| слово   | word     |
| место   | place    |
| море    | sea      |
| здание  | building |

### The Soft Sign: ь

Nouns ending in ь can be masculine or feminine.

Examples:

| Russian | Meaning    | Gender    |
| ------- | ---------- | --------- |
| день    | day        | Masculine |
| словарь | dictionary | Masculine |
| дверь   | door       | Feminine  |
| ночь    | night      | Feminine  |

**Important:** Do not assume that every noun ending in ь is feminine.

For these nouns, memorize the gender together with the word.

---

## Lesson 3.3 — Why Gender Matters

Gender becomes especially important when words agree with nouns.

Consider:

новый стол — a new table
новая книга — a new book
новое окно — a new window

The adjective новый changes according to the gender of the noun.

You do not need to master adjective declension today. That will come in Week 3.

For now, understand the principle:

**The noun's gender influences the form of related words.**

This is why learning a noun together with its gender is a valuable habit.

---

## Lesson 3.4 — Personal Pronouns and Gender

Russian uses он, она, and оно to refer to nouns according to grammatical gender.

| Russian | Meaning                  |
| ------- | ------------------------ |
| Он      | He / it (masculine noun) |
| Она     | She / it (feminine noun) |
| Оно     | It (neuter noun)         |

Examples:

Стол. Он большой.
A table. It is big.

Книга. Она интересная.
A book. It is interesting.

Окно. Оно большое.
A window. It is big.

These examples introduce adjective agreement, which you will study more fully in Week 3.

For today, focus on the connection between noun gender and the pronouns он, она, and оно.

# 4. DAY 3 — Present-Tense Verbs: First Conjugation

## Today's objective

Yesterday, you learned to describe people and things using nouns. Today, you will learn to describe **actions**.

This is the beginning of productive Russian grammar.

You will learn to say:

* I live in Kathmandu.
* You speak Russian.
* She works.
* We know.

## Lesson 4.1 — What Is Conjugation?

In English, the verb usually changes very little:

* I work.
* You work.
* We work.
* They work.
* He works.

Russian verbs change their endings according to the subject.

For example, the verb работать means "to work."

| Russian       | English    |
| ------------- | ---------- |
| Я работаю.    | I work.    |
| Ты работаешь. | You work.  |
| Он работает.  | He works.  |
| Мы работаем.  | We work.   |
| Вы работаете. | You work.  |
| Они работают. | They work. |

The verb ending tells you who is performing the action.

This process is called **conjugation**.

---

## Lesson 4.2 — The Infinitive

The infinitive is the basic dictionary form of a verb.

Examples:

| Russian  | English  |
| -------- | -------- |
| работать | to work  |
| читать   | to read  |
| думать   | to think |
| знать    | to know  |
| жить     | to live  |
| говорить | to speak |

Many Russian infinitives end in -ть.

However, infinitive endings alone do not always tell you which conjugation pattern a verb follows.

For now, learn each verb together with its present-tense forms.

---

## Lesson 4.3 — First-Conjugation Pattern

A common first-conjugation pattern uses these endings:

| Pronoun        | Ending    | Example: читать |
| -------------- | --------- | --------------- |
| Я              | -ю / -у   | читаю           |
| Ты             | -ешь      | читаешь         |
| Он / Она / Оно | -ет       | читает          |
| Мы             | -ем       | читаем          |
| Вы             | -ете      | читаете         |
| Они            | -ют / -ут | читают          |

The verb читать means "to read."

### Full Conjugation

| Russian     | English    |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| Я читаю.    | I read.    |
| Ты читаешь. | You read.  |
| Он читает.  | He reads.  |
| Она читает. | She reads. |
| Мы читаем.  | We read.   |
| Вы читаете. | You read.  |
| Они читают. | They read. |

### Instructor's Note

The endings above represent a common first-conjugation pattern. Russian has spelling rules and exceptions, and not every verb ending in -ать follows this exact pattern.

Learn the pattern first, then learn exceptions as you encounter them.

---

## Lesson 4.4 — Three Useful Verbs

### Читать — To Read

| Russian     | English    |
| ----------- | ---------- |
| Я читаю.    | I read.    |
| Ты читаешь. | You read.  |
| Он читает.  | He reads.  |
| Мы читаем.  | We read.   |
| Они читают. | They read. |

### Работать — To Work

| Russian       | English    |
| ------------- | ---------- |
| Я работаю.    | I work.    |
| Ты работаешь. | You work.  |
| Он работает.  | He works.  |
| Мы работаем.  | We work.   |
| Они работают. | They work. |

### Знать — To Know

| Russian    | English    |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| Я знаю.    | I know.    |
| Ты знаешь. | You know.  |
| Он знает.  | He knows.  |
| Мы знаем.  | We know.   |
| Они знают. | They know. |

Знать follows the first-conjugation pattern.

---

## Lesson 4.5 — Using Verbs in Sentences

Now combine pronouns, verbs, and simple nouns.

Я читаю.
I read.

Я работаю.
I work.

Я знаю.
I know.

Она работает.
She works.

Мы читаем.
We read.

Notice that Russian often allows the pronoun to be omitted when the verb ending already makes the subject clear.

For example:

**Я читаю. → Читаю.**

Both can mean "I read."

At this stage, keep the pronoun in your practice sentences. This will help reinforce the conjugation patterns.

# 5. DAY 4 — Present-Tense Verbs: Second Conjugation

## Today's objective

Today, you will learn another major present-tense conjugation pattern.

You will also learn to combine verbs with the personal information introduced earlier in the week.

---

## Lesson 5.1 — Second-Conjugation Pattern

A common second-conjugation pattern uses these endings:

| Pronoun        | Ending    | Example: говорить |
| -------------- | --------- | ----------------- |
| Я              | -ю / -у   | говорю            |
| Ты             | -ишь      | говоришь          |
| Он / Она / Оно | -ит       | говорит           |
| Мы             | -им       | говорим           |
| Вы             | -ите      | говорите          |
| Они            | -ят / -ат | говорят           |

Говорить means "to speak."

### Full Conjugation

| Russian      | English     |
| ------------ | ----------- |
| Я говорю.    | I speak.    |
| Ты говоришь. | You speak.  |
| Он говорит.  | He speaks.  |
| Она говорит. | She speaks. |
| Мы говорим.  | We speak.   |
| Вы говорите. | You speak.  |
| Они говорят. | They speak. |

The verb говорить is a common second-conjugation verb.

Many verbs ending in -ить follow this pattern, although there are exceptions and additional classification rules.

---

## Lesson 5.2 — Жить: To Live

Жить is an important verb for talking about where you live.

| Russian    | English    |
| ---------- | ---------- |
| Я живу.    | I live.    |
| Ты живёшь. | You live.  |
| Он живёт.  | He lives.  |
| Мы живём.  | We live.   |
| Вы живёте. | You live.  |
| Они живут. | They live. |

Notice that the stem changes in spelling and pronunciation.

Do not try to derive every form mechanically from the infinitive. Learn the complete conjugation as a pattern.

---

## Lesson 5.3 — Говорить: To Speak

Use говорить when discussing languages.

Я говорю по-русски.
I speak Russian.

Ты говоришь по-английски.
You speak English.

Она говорит по-русски.
She speaks Russian.

Мы говорим по-английски.
We speak English.

### Important Vocabulary

| Russian      | English    |
| ------------ | ---------- |
| по-русски    | in Russian |
| по-английски | in English |
| по-непальски | in Nepali  |

These expressions are useful when describing the language in which someone speaks.

For now, memorize them as complete vocabulary items.

---

## Lesson 5.4 — First vs. Second Conjugation

Compare:

| Pronoun  | Читать  | Говорить |
| -------- | ------- | -------- |
| Я        | читаю   | говорю   |
| Ты       | читаешь | говоришь |
| Он / Она | читает  | говорит  |
| Мы       | читаем  | говорим  |
| Вы       | читаете | говорите |
| Они      | читают  | говорят  |

### The Pattern to Notice

First conjugation:

* Ты читаешь.
* Он читает.
* Мы читаем.

Second conjugation:

* Ты говоришь.
* Он говорит.
* Мы говорим.

The endings differ.

You do not need to memorize every conjugation classification rule yet. The priority is recognizing and using common verbs correctly.

---

## Lesson 5.5 — Negation with Verbs

The negative particle не comes before the verb.

Я говорю.
I speak.

Я не говорю.
I do not speak.

Она работает.
She works.

Она не работает.
She does not work.

Мы читаем.
We read.

Мы не читаем.
We do not read.

### Pattern

**Subject + не + conjugated verb**

Я не говорю по-русски.

I do not speak Russian.

This pattern works with the verbs you have learned so far.

Я говорю по-русски.
I speak Russian.

Я говорю по-английски и по-непальски.
I speak English and Nepali.

**и** means "and."

### Important Distinction

Russian has two common ways to talk about languages:

* говорить по-русски — to speak Russian
* говорить на русском языке — to speak in the Russian language

Both are correct, but the first is a useful beginner pattern to learn.

## Lesson 6.5 — Asking About Languages

### Ты говоришь по-русски?

Do you speak Russian? (informal)

### Вы говорите по-английски?

Do you speak English? (formal)

Russian commonly forms simple yes/no questions using the same word order as a statement, with question intonation and context.

Compare:

Ты говоришь по-русски.
You speak Russian.

Ты говоришь по-русски?
Do you speak Russian?

The written question mark and spoken intonation help indicate the question.

## Lesson 7.1 — The Basic Russian Sentence

A simple Russian sentence can follow this structure:

**Subject + Verb + Other Information**

Examples:

Я живу в Катманду.
I live in Kathmandu.

Я говорю по-английски.
I speak English.

Она работает в городе.
She works in the city.

Мы читаем книги.
We read books.

Russian word order is flexible, but this structure is a useful starting point.

For now, use subject–verb–object or subject–verb–complement patterns when constructing your own sentences.

---

## Lesson 7.2 — Combining Identity and Action

You can now combine two types of sentences.

### Identity

Я студент.
I am a student.

### Action

Я учусь.
I study.

The verb учиться means "to study" or "to learn."

It is a reflexive verb and has a different conjugation pattern. For this week, learn Я учусь as a complete expression. Reflexive verbs will be studied in more detail in Week 6.

Other examples:

Я работаю.
I work.

Я живу в Катманду.
I live in Kathmandu.

Я говорю по-английски.
I speak English.

## Week 2 — Instructor's Final Guidance

This week introduces a fundamental shift: you are no longer limited to repeating memorized phrases. You are learning how to construct new Russian sentences.

The three most important things to retain are:

1. **Russian usually omits "am/is/are" in the present tense.**
2. **Every Russian noun has a grammatical gender.**
3. **Russian verbs change their endings according to the subject.**

Do not worry if you still need time to recall verb forms. Repeated use in meaningful sentences is what makes them automatic.
